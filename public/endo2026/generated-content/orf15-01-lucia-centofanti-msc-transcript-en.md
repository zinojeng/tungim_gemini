Importantly, this effect appeared to be independent of the classical 5-HT2A receptor in the central nervous system and were instead associated with antagonism of the hepatic serotonin receptor, 5-HT2B. Based on these findings, we aim to develop a non-psychoactive psilocin derivative capable of maintaining all the metabolic benefits while eliminating the central nervous system exposure. Specifically, we investigated a pegylated version of psilocin with eight molecules of PEG. To evaluate its efficacy, a mouse MASLD model was created on male C57BL/6 mice fed with a high-fat, high-fructose diet for 17 weeks. Mice with a standard diet were used as healthy controls, while one group of MASLD mice was treated with a low dose of psilocybin and another with an equivalent dose of PEG-8-psilocin. First of all, PEG-8-psilocin was well tolerated and, importantly, the pegylation nearly eliminated the blood-brain barrier penetration while retaining its activity at serotonin receptor, showing a pharmacological profile comparable to the one of psilocybin. PEG-8-psilocin showed even greater metabolic effects than psilocybin itself. Treatment significantly reduced body weight gain, normalized glucose tolerance, reduced insulin resistance as assessed by the HOMA-IR, and improved beta cell function as reflected by the HOMA-B values.
