## Aggressive thyroid cancer and keratin 5

**Claudia Wahoski:** I invite everyone in the audience to find their collarbone and then move their hands approximately one inch up the neck. This is the location of the thyroid, an important organ that controls general metabolic function, among many other things. My research focuses on thyroid cancer.

Thyroid cancer is the most common endocrine malignancy, and the number of people diagnosed each year has increased over the past several decades. Fortunately, most cases are treatable. Some patients, however, develop aggressive and lethal disease that is very difficult to treat. A major problem in the field is identifying the biological processes that drive aggressive disease and the patients at risk of developing it.

We have found that some thyroid cancers contain aggressive, invasive cells that express keratin 5 (KRT5). Keratin 5–positive cells have also been identified in cancers such as breast and ovarian cancer. Research has associated a greater presence of keratin 5–positive cells with poor outcomes, including treatment resistance and metastasis. If these cells are linked to poor outcomes in other cancers, it is important to understand their role and origin in the thyroid.

## Relationship between WNT signaling and keratin 5

My project focuses on understanding why thyroid cancers become aggressive. Using a diverse thyroid-cancer patient cohort curated at Vanderbilt University, our laboratory studies aggressive thyroid tumors.

In a patient's tumor containing areas of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) that had progressed to anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC), the aggressive ATC component showed markedly increased keratin 5 expression. Another characteristic we have identified in aggressive thyroid tumors is high WNT/β-catenin signaling activity. We did not know how keratin 5 and WNT signaling were related.

I therefore treated thyroid-cancer cells with a WNT-pathway activator. Cells with increased WNT activation also showed increased keratin 5 expression. This is important because WNT signaling is potentially targetable. The working hypothesis is that inhibiting WNT could reduce keratin 5 expression and aggressive tumor behavior, but these data are preclinical and do not establish a clinical treatment.

Thank you.

**Claudia Wahoski:** I have been treating thyroid-cancer cells with WNT inhibitors and examining whether keratin 5 expression decreases. One problem is that WNT is an essential pathway, so inhibition can simply kill the cells. That may be useful for tumor treatment, but we are still dissecting the relationship between WNT signaling and keratin 5.

**Audience member:** Regarding keratin 5, the current data sound strongly correlative. Have you tested increased and decreased expression functionally? Is keratin 5 sufficient or required for the aggressive phenotype?
