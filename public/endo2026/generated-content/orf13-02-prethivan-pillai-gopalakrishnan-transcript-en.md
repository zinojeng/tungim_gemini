## Clinical need and study aim

Good afternoon, everyone. I am Prethivan Gopalakrishnan, and I am going to present our study, “Quantitative Metadrenaline Burden and Tumor Characteristics Predict Prognosis in Phaeochromocytoma and Paraganglioma: A 12-Year Single Centre Cohort Study.”

Risk stratification in patients with pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma (PPGL) remains challenging. We have several scores available, such as PASS, GAPP, SGAP, and COPPS, but their predictive accuracy is moderate. Genetic testing is extremely important and is included in many of these scores, but unfortunately it is not performed for every patient; even when it is performed, the result can be delayed.

Metadrenalines are crucial to the diagnosis of secretory PPGL, but biochemical burden itself is not incorporated into these scores. When metadrenaline results are included, they are generally treated in a binary fashion—elevated or not elevated—rather than according to how elevated they are.

We therefore wanted to assess whether biochemical burden, which we called BioMax, together with other routine clinical features, could predict metastasis and all-cause mortality in patients with PPGL.

## Cohort and biochemical assessment

Our consecutive cohort included 100 patients from 2012 to 2023. After applying the exclusion criteria, 80 patients were analyzed: 70 had pheochromocytoma and 10 had paraganglioma.

Plasma metadrenaline levels were analyzed by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) and normalized to the upper limit of normal (ULN). The maximum fold above the ULN among normetadrenaline, metadrenaline, and 3-methoxytyramine (3-MT) was selected as BioMax. This provides an assay-independent measure of the dominant secretory activity.

The results were analyzed using Firth penalized logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis with 1,000-bootstrap validation. Youden analysis was used to determine the optimal BioMax cutoff for predicting metastasis and the optimal tumor-size cutoff.

## Development of the NBS score

We assessed several variables: BioMax, central necrosis on histopathology, tumor size, Ki-67 index, age, and sex. Youden analysis identified a BioMax cutoff of at least six times the ULN and a tumor-size cutoff of at least 7 cm.

In our cohort, only necrosis, BioMax, and tumor size were significant. We incorporated these three variables into a simple Necrosis–BioMax–Size (NBS) score ranging from 0 to 3 to predict metastasis and all-cause mortality.

## Cohort outcomes and metastatic phenotype

The median follow-up was four years, with an interquartile range of two to seven years. The median age at diagnosis was 59 years, and 54% of patients were male. Metastasis occurred in 13% overall, with similar rates among patients with pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma in our cohort.

Aggressive tumors were larger, more likely to be necrotic, and had a higher biochemical burden. Among patients with metastatic disease, tumor size was significantly larger, necrosis was more common, and BioMax was higher.

When the individual plasma metadrenalines were evaluated, normetadrenaline had 100% sensitivity but low specificity. 3-MT was more specific than sensitive, whereas metadrenaline was neither sensitive nor specific.

## NBS discrimination and mortality

For prediction of metastasis, the NBS score showed excellent discrimination, with an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.96. At a threshold of 2 or more, it had 90% sensitivity, 90% specificity, and 90% accuracy, with a high negative predictive value of 98%. Metastatic risk increased as the score increased.

All-cause mortality in our cohort was approximately 19%. Patients with metastatic disease and those with a higher NBS score of 2 or more were more likely to have a higher mortality rate.

## Clinical implications and conclusion

BioMax may reflect an aggressive PPGL phenotype and could prompt earlier use of functional imaging, particularly ^68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT or DOPA PET/CT. This may be especially useful in centers where functional imaging is not routinely incorporated into the diagnostic evaluation of PPGL and MIBG is usually used.

BioMax can provide crucial information even preoperatively because it requires only the degree of biochemical elevation. In our cohort, a BioMax of at least six times the ULN was associated with a high likelihood of metastasis. This does not replace genetic testing; it is an additional variable that may help us manage patients.

Earlier detection of metastatic disease enables accurate staging, prognostic stratification, consideration of radionuclide therapy, and tailored surveillance. Unlike existing models, the NBS score offers a simple yet strong prognostic tool for timely surveillance. External validation in larger multicenter cohorts is definitely needed, and we are currently working on that. Thank you.
