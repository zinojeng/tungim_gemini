## 02:28–09:01 — Ana Claudia Latronico

### Definition and pubertal onset

**Ana Claudia Latronico:** Good afternoon. Central precocious puberty is traditionally defined by the development of secondary sexual characteristics before age 8 years in girls and age 9 years in boys. It results from premature activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis. In fact, central precocious puberty represents a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)-dependent disorder.

In girls, pubertal onset is defined clinically by breast development at Tanner stage B2, whereas achieving a testicular volume ≥4 mL or length >2.5 cm defines pubertal onset in boys.

### Epidemiology and clinical diagnosis

Central precocious puberty is a neuroendocrine disorder with an increased incidence globally and is 10 times more common in girls than boys. The incidence of central precocious puberty ranges from 0.2 to 26 per 10,000 girls per year and 0.02 to 0.9 per 10,000 boys per year.

The clinical diagnosis of CPP is based on a history of progressive premature sexual development associated with accelerated growth velocity, >6 cm per year, and advanced bone age, >1 year. Pubertal concentrations of basal or GnRH-stimulated luteinizing hormone (LH) confirm the diagnosis, with normal or elevated sex steroids: estradiol in girls and testosterone in boys.

### Potential causes and classification

The potential causes of central precocious puberty are usually evaluated by brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The prevalence of structural intracranial lesions has been reported to be much higher in boys than in girls with CPP, and it decreases after age 6 years in girls.

Recognition of genetic factors in the underlying mechanisms of central precocious puberty has grown significantly over the last decade, as demonstrated by the identification of monogenic causes, especially in familial central precocious puberty. The current classification of central precocious puberty is organic, genetic, and idiopathic.

### Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis of premature sexual development due to CPP includes early normal puberty, especially borderline cases in girls between 6 and 8 years; common variants of premature pubertal development, including isolated precocious thelarche and adrenarche; and other causes of peripheral precocious puberty.

### Evaluation and treatment goals

Among the several goals in the evaluation and treatment of CPP are inducing regression of secondary sexual characteristics, preserving genetic adult-height potential, preventing early menarche, promoting the psychosocial well-being of patients and family members, and detecting and treating a possible intracranial expansive lesion.

### GnRH agonist treatment

GnRH agonists (GnRHa) have been established as the first-line treatment of central precocious puberty since the 1980s. Use of GnRH agonists is associated with regression or stabilization of pubertal development, reduction of growth velocity, slowing of bone-age advancement, and preservation of adult-height potential.

Several GnRH agonists are currently available in various depot forms, and their approval for use and recommended doses in central precocious puberty vary across different countries.

### Guideline scope and subgroup analyses

In this novel guideline, our focus was on diagnostic evaluation and treatment considerations. Ten controversial clinical questions were prioritized, and three to seven health-related outcomes were selected for each.

Subgroup analyses for a subset of clinical questions were performed according to age in both sexes. For example, the subgroups were age <6 versus 6.0–8.0 years in girls and age <8 versus 8.0–9.0 years in boys.
