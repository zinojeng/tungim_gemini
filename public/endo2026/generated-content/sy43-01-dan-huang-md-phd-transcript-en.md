## ADP-ribosylation and PARP1 biology

My research focuses on ADP-ribosylation, which is a post-translational modification of proteins. Unlike other well-established PTMs, such as methylation or acetylation, I think ADP-ribosylation is less well understood.

During this modification, PARP enzymes can transfer ADP-ribose from NAD+ to a variety of acceptor or substrate proteins, resulting in covalent attachment of either a single ADP-ribose, which we call MAR, or polymers of ADP-ribose, which can also be called PAR.

ADP-ribosylation is a dynamic and well-regulated system. It can be regulated by writers, such as the PARPs, which add this ADP-ribosylation, and it can be reversibly removed by erasers such as PARG and ARH3. It's very interesting that nature creates these natural protein domains that have different affinities to bind and recognize different forms of ADP-ribose.

As NAD+ is the substrate of ADP-ribosylation, the biosynthesis of NAD+ is very critical for ADP-ribosylation. Enzymes responsible for NAD+ synthesis, such as the NMNAT enzyme family, are defined as the feeders of ADP-ribosylation.

PARP enzymes are a family of mono- or poly(ADP-ribose) transferases consisting of 17 different members. They have different structures, subcellular localizations, enzymatic activities, and biological functions.

My research focuses on PARP1, which is the founding member of the PARP family and the major contributor to PARylation in cells. PARP1 accounts for about 80% of PARylation in cells. It is localized in the nucleus and associated with chromatin. It plays critical roles in many nuclear processes, such as DNA repair and DNA replication.

These two molecular mechanisms led to the discovery and development of PARP inhibitors. So far, four different PARP inhibitors have been approved by the FDA to treat breast cancer, ovarian cancer, and prostate cancer. The activity of a PARP inhibitor is mostly targeting PARP1 and PARP2, and it acts in combination with homologous-recombination deficiency, such as a BRCA mutation, which leads to synthetic lethality in the cancer cell.

Besides DNA-damage responses, PARP1 plays critical roles in other nuclear processes, including transcription. Work from my lab and other labs provides strong evidence showing that PARP1 and PARP1-mediated PARylation are central to transcriptional regulation of many biological processes through different transcription factors, including nuclear receptors.

They can regulate cardiac remodeling, vascular fibrosis, adipogenesis, and liver disease. Importantly, they can regulate immunity and inflammation through inflammatory transcription factors such as STAT1α and NF-κB.

## NAD+ synthesis and signal-responsive transcription

The activity of PARP enzymes is tightly related to synthesis of NAD+, which is consumed in the ADP-ribosylation reaction and must be regenerated. In mammalian cells, the salvage pathway that uses nicotinamide to synthesize NAD+ is critical for regulating NAD+-dependent PARP activity and cell function.

NMNAT enzymes catalyze the last step in this synthesis pathway, converting NMN with ATP into NAD+. In cells, NMNAT has three different versions. They are localized in distinct cellular compartments: NMNAT1 is located in the nucleus, NMNAT2 is located in the cytosol and Golgi, and NMNAT3 is located in the mitochondria. Different NMNAT enzymes are responsible for subcellular NAD+ pools.

NAD+-dependent PARP1 activation can respond to extracellular signaling pathways, including steroid hormones, polypeptide hormones, and inflammatory signals. In response to signaling pathways, PARP1 can interact with or directly modify transcription factors to reprogram transcriptional outcomes.

Those transcription factors include steroid nuclear receptors such as ERα and the progesterone receptor, and other nonsteroid nuclear receptors such as PPARα and PPARγ. We also found that another transcription factor in the bZIP family, C/EBPβ, is a substrate for PARP1-mediated PARylation.

PARP1-dependent regulation of transcription factors plays key roles in a variety of biological processes. Today I will tell you two stories: how PARP1 mediates immunometabolic signaling in obesity, focusing on the role of PARP1 in macrophages; and how PARP1 mediates hormonal signaling in pregnancy to regulate cardiac function.

## Macrophage PARP1 in adipose-tissue homeostasis and obesity

In my first story, I identified PARP1 as a tissue-specific regulator in adipose tissue. We know that adipose tissue is a great contributor to obesity and obesity-related metabolic consequences. But adipose tissue is very heterogeneous. It consists of many cell populations. Besides adipocytes, there are adipocyte precursors and a range of different immune-cell populations, including macrophages, T cells, and NK cells. Here I show that interactions between different immune-cell populations can contribute to regulation of adipose tissue.

Previously, my work showed that PARP1 in specific preadipocytes, the adipocyte precursors, can control the adipogenesis process. Knockout of PARP1 in preadipocytes dramatically enhances differentiation of preadipocytes into newly synthesized mature adipocytes, and this leads to enhanced obesity upon high-fat-diet treatment in mice.

Adipose-tissue macrophages are the predominant immune-cell type in adipose tissue and are critical contributors to obesity. My next question was: what is the role of macrophage PARP1 in regulation of the adipose-tissue inflammatory and metabolic environment? And what are the effects of macrophage PARP1 in mediating cell–cell interactions, such as how macrophages regulate preadipocytes and adipocytes, and how macrophages alter immune-cell crosstalk?

I will not go into too much detail, but we generated macrophage-specific PARP1-knockout mice and treated them with a high-fat diet for about 12 weeks. What we observed is that macrophage-specific PARP1-knockout mice show enhanced adipocyte hypertrophy and expansion, and we also observed increased macrophage infiltration.

Finally, we observed phenotypes similar to PARP1 knockout in preadipocytes: macrophage-specific PARP1 knockout can enhance high-fat-diet-induced obesity and eventually cause insulin resistance in mice.

How does PARP1 in macrophages regulate this adipose-tissue expansion? First, we wanted to look at crosstalk between macrophages and preadipocytes. We used conditioned medium collected from bone-marrow-derived macrophages from either PARP1 wild-type or knockout mice, treated those macrophages with LPS, and then used the conditioned medium during preadipocyte differentiation.

What we observed is that when PARP1 is knocked out, it dramatically decreases proinflammatory cytokine expression as well as secretion into the culture medium. Using this medium, we observed dramatically enhanced adipogenesis and adipogenic gene expression during preadipocyte differentiation, indicating that suppression of proinflammatory cytokines by PARP1 depletion in macrophages actually enhances adipogenesis. This is in line with previous studies showing that PARP1 is a critical mediator of inflammatory responses in macrophages.

Most of the cytokines are downregulated, but one cytokine, IL-27, changes in the opposite direction. It increases upon PARP1 knockdown or knockout in cells and tissues. What is the role of IL-27? When we treated preadipocytes with IL-27 during the differentiation process and compared the effects of IL-27 on adipocytes, we observed that IL-27 has dual effects. It not only enhances adipogenesis, but it also induces expression and secretion of many adipokines and cytokines that lead to adverse metabolic outcomes in mice.

Finally, we wanted to see immune-cell interactions by single-cell sequencing. We found that when PARP1 is knocked out in macrophages, it alters the cellular composition. It increases the proportions of macrophages, NK cells, and monocytes, and decreases B cells and T cells. Cell-communication analysis showed that PARP1 knockout in macrophages dramatically enhances interactions between NK cells and macrophages.

From this part of my story, I identified PARP1 as a key regulator in maintaining white-adipose-tissue homeostasis. Absence of PARP1 in macrophages promotes obesity and metabolic dysfunction by altering interactions among immune cells as well as interactions between macrophages and adipocytes.

## Cardiomyocyte PARP1 and maternal cardiac adaptation

Let's dive into another story. Here we focus on cardiomyocyte PARP1 and how it mediates hormonal signaling in maternal cardiac adaptation during pregnancy.

Pregnancy is a fascinating physiological process driven by many hormonal signals. Pregnancy can trigger dramatic surges of steroid hormones that increase and peak before term. This fluctuation in hormones is critical for fetal development, but at the same time it adapts the maternal body to pregnancy.

For example, during pregnancy, the maternal immune system undergoes compositional changes in response to hormonal signaling. Neutrophils and monocytes increase, and T cells and NK cells decrease. T-helper-cell categories also switch phenotype.

What I'm very interested in as a cardiologist is how the heart changes during pregnancy. We took hearts from nonpregnant mice and late-pregnant mice. By eye, you can observe obvious changes in the heart. In late pregnancy, the maternal heart undergoes physiological hypertrophy and enlargement. This is represented by enlargement of the left ventricle and increased left-ventricular wall thickness, as well as increases in heart rate, cardiac output, and blood volume. All these changes meet the increased demand from the mother and the baby.

Energy for the heart is mostly derived from fatty acids and glucose. In late pregnancy, to meet the increased demand of hypertrophy in the heart, cardiac metabolism is remodeled. There is further enhancement of fatty-acid oxidation, moderately enhanced use of lactate and ketones as energy substrates, and decreased glucose oxidation.

Why am I very interested in the heart? When you take a look at this blot, you can appreciate that across all the tissues we tested from mice, the cardiovascular system—such as the heart and artery—as well as skeletal muscle actually has the highest PARylation, by many-fold over other tissues. The Human Protein Atlas shows that PARP1 is the major PARP enzyme in heart tissue. We hypothesized that PARP1-mediated PARylation plays a critical role in regulating cardiac function.

To study cardiomyocyte PARP1 and its effects on cardiac adaptation during pregnancy, we generated cardiomyocyte-specific PARP1-knockout mice. These are tamoxifen-inducible mice. We confirmed PARP1 knockout in the knockout mice, and we do see a reduction in PARylation in cardiac tissue. Then we generated this pregnancy model and assessed cardiac function as well as pregnancy outcomes in the mice.

First, I will show you how the hearts look in wild-type and knockout mice. In late pregnancy, you see a decreased size of the heart when PARP1 is knocked out. Heart weight is decreased dramatically. If you take a closer look at individual cardiomyocytes, you see a smaller size of individual cardiomyocytes, but this is accompanied by increased fibrosis in the heart. This suggests that inadequate cardiac adaptation or hypertrophy may lead to cardiac dysfunction.

Indeed, we assessed the echocardiogram. In nonpregnant mice there is no significant difference, but in late-pregnant mice we see a dramatic reduction in cardiac function, represented by ejection fraction and fractional-shortening percentage.

## Hormone-regulated gene expression and cardiac metabolism

The next question is how progesterone regulates cardiac function during pregnancy. To answer this question, we need to understand the substrates. We did immunoprecipitation to pull down all ADP-ribosylated proteins in cardiac tissue, and we submitted these ADP-ribosylated substrates for mass-spectrometry analysis. It showed that most PARP1-mediated ADP-ribosylation substrates are enriched in transcriptional regulation and chromatin regulation.

Now we have confidence to study progesterone gene regulation in cardiomyocytes during pregnancy. We did RNA sequencing for cardiac tissue in late-pregnant mice. What we observed was a dramatic alteration in gene expression. The altered genes are enriched in transcriptional regulation, signal transduction, and immune-response regulation.

We observed that two genes were highly regulated. One is Nppb, which encodes BNP, a marker of heart failure. This indicates that PARP1 knockout will induce cardiomyocyte dysfunction. We also observed that Esr1, which encodes ERα, increased in this dataset, suggesting that PARP1 may mediate hormone-signaling-regulated gene expression during these processes.

Further analysis of the downregulated gene set found that a subset of PARP1-downregulated genes are predicted steroid-nuclear-receptor target genes and are highly enriched in metabolic terms such as fatty-acid metabolism.

As I mentioned earlier, fatty-acid oxidation is enhanced in the heart during late pregnancy. But how does it behave when PARP1 is knocked out? The genes that have changed indicate decreased fatty-acid oxidation and increased glucose oxidation. This indicates reversal of this metabolic remodeling, and this could be one mechanism that causes heart failure or cardiac dysfunction, as indicated by the heart-failure marker genes.

ERα is highly upregulated in our gene set, so we wanted to see how PARP1 knockout or hormonal changes increase or regulate ERα expression as well as DNA binding. We saw that during late pregnancy, ERα protein expression also increases in cardiac tissue. Knockout of PARP1 actually enhances ERα DNA binding at different genomic loci, including some classical ERα target genes such as Greb1 and Pgr. We also observed that ERα can bind directly to promoters of the Nppa and Nppb genes that are responsible for regulating cardiac function.

Because the progesterone receptor is also upregulated by ERα binding, we wanted to see how progesterone-receptor expression and DNA binding are altered by PARP1 knockout. Although the protein does not change too much during pregnancy in mouse heart, we do see in late pregnancy increased binding of PR-A and PARP1 in the chromatin fraction, indicating that interaction between PR and PARP1 may play a key role in regulating PR DNA binding and gene regulation.

ChIP-qPCR for PR showed that PARP1 knockout can regulate PR DNA binding at specific gene loci. These two genes are related to fatty-acid oxidation.

In summary, cardiomyocyte PARP1 mediates hormone-regulated gene expression required for cardiac adaptation during pregnancy. This project is still going on. We are now trying to understand genomically the regulatory effects of PARP1 on hormone-regulated gene expression.

## Pregnancy outcomes and maternal–fetal crosstalk

Next, we want to see how altered maternal cardiac function from PARP1 knockout impacts pregnancy outcomes. What we observed is that maternal PARP1 knockout leads to smaller fetuses: the fetuses are smaller and weigh less. The proportion of small-for-gestational-age fetuses is higher in the knockout group.

When you look at the placenta and developing fetuses, you see fewer developing fetuses and higher embryonic resorption in knockout mice. Importantly, when we assayed placentas collected from wild-type and knockout mice, we observed significant changes in expression of some metabolic genes in the placenta.

In summary, this part of my work indicates that PARP1 knockout specifically in cardiomyocytes of the maternal heart can induce impaired cardiac adaptation, including impaired cardiac function, metabolic switching, and hormone-regulated gene expression. This inadequate cardiac adaptation can crosstalk with the placenta and fetuses, leading to adverse pregnancy outcomes.

As a next step, we want to figure out the direct link between these two organs mediated through PARP1 in the pregnancy model.
