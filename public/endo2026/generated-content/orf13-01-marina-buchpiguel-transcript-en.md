## Background and objective

Good afternoon, everybody. My name is Marina Buchpiguel, and today I will be presenting the abstract “Expanding the Clinical, Genetic, and Histopathological Spectrum of Primary Pigmented Nodular Adrenocortical Disease: Insights from a Brazilian Referral Cohort.”

Primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease, or PPNAD, is a rare cause of ACTH-independent Cushing syndrome that is frequently associated with Carney complex. There are limited integrated clinical and molecular data available. Our objective was therefore to investigate the clinicopathologic and molecular heterogeneity of PPNAD with or without Carney complex.

## Cohort and molecular methods

We collected both retrospective and prospective data from a tertiary referral center in Brazil and gathered 18 index cases. Through cascade genetic testing, we substantially expanded the number of affected or at-risk individuals. This allowed us to evaluate not only clinically apparent disease, but also milder phenotypes and asymptomatic carriers, producing a final cohort of 38 individuals who underwent clinical, hormonal, radiological, and molecular evaluation.

For molecular analysis, we performed next-generation sequencing (NGS), whole-exome sequencing, copy-number variation (CNV) analysis, and somatic analysis when tissue was available.

## Clinical characteristics and surgical management

Among our original 18 index patients, 88% were female, and the mean age at diagnosis was 24 years. The adrenal status at baseline was mostly overt Cushing syndrome; 17% had cyclic Cushing syndrome, and no index patient had mild autonomous cortisol secretion (MACS). MACS was present only among variant-positive relatives.

Regarding surgical management, 72% underwent bilateral adrenalectomy, 11% underwent staged unilateral adrenalectomy, and 17% remained under clinical surveillance.

Among our index patients with Carney complex (CNC), extra-adrenal manifestations were fairly common. The most frequent were hyperprolactinemia, acromegaly, skin lentigines, benign breast nodules, breast carcinomas, and the classic cardiac myxomas.

## Germline and somatic findings

The main molecular finding was the identification of germline *PRKAR1A* variants in 66% of our index cases, including four likely novel variants. We also identified two germline *PRKACA* duplications, reinforcing the central role of cAMP–PKA pathway dysregulation. Even among individuals with identical variants, we found very different clinical phenotypes.

Sixteen percent of our probands were gene-negative, suggesting the possible involvement of non-coding variants, mosaicism, or other epigenetic mechanisms.

Somatic analysis was feasible in only 13 adrenalectomized patients. Second-hit alterations were present in only 23% of these patients, and no recurrent somatic driver was identified. This suggests that the classic two-hit model may not fully explain disease expression.

## Histopathological spectrum

Most patients presented with the classic PPNAD histological pattern, with micronodules, prominent lipofuscin accumulation, and distinct internodular cortical atrophy. However, three patients had non-classical histology, with either mixed micro- and macronodular disease or diffuse cortical hyperplasia.

## Conclusions

Taken together, our findings support the view that PPNAD and Carney complex represent a dynamic clinicomolecular spectrum rather than a uniform genetic condition. Although dysregulation of the cAMP–PKA pathway appears to be the main pathogenetic mechanism, we encountered very broad phenotypic variability, even among patients with the same variant, suggesting limited genotype–phenotype predictability.

In addition, somatic second-hit events were inconsistently identified across tissues. This suggests that haploinsufficiency may be sufficient in some cases and that somatic events may act only as phenotype modulators.

We also identified histological overlap between micro- and macronodular patterns. MACS was identified only through cascade screening, suggesting that subclinical and early-stage disease may be underrecognized.

Overall, our findings reinforce the importance of multidisciplinary follow-up and systematic family screening in all patients with PPNAD and CNC. Thank you very much.
