## Introduction and overview

**Moderator:** Our final speaker is Dr. Katharina Wang from LMU University Hospital in Munich. She will present “Bedside-Bench-Back: Personalized Drug Testing in PPGL.”

**Katharina Wang:** Thank you very much. I am very happy to present our work on personalized drug testing in pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma—bedside, bench, and back—and I am also representing my mentor, Svenja Nölting, today.

I will review the background and molecular clusters of pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas, collectively called PPGLs; discuss systemic therapies currently used in practice; and introduce our patient-derived PPGL primary-culture platform for personalized drug testing, drug repurposing, and individualized risk assessment.

## Genetics and molecular clusters of PPGL

PPGLs have the highest heritability of all tumors. Germline pathogenic variants are found in approximately 30% to 40%, and somatic pathogenic variants in tumor tissue in another 35% to 40%. As a result, approximately 65% to 80% of PPGLs can be assigned to one of three major molecular clusters according to their genetic background.

Cluster 1 is the pseudohypoxia-related cluster and includes pathogenic variants in SDHx genes, VHL, and EPAS1, which encodes HIF-2α. Cluster 2 includes alterations in kinase-signaling pathways, such as NF1 and RET. Cluster 3 contains alterations in Wnt-signaling pathways and is less common than clusters 1 and 2.

Cluster 1 tumors are more often extra-adrenal and have a higher metastatic risk, reaching up to 75% in some genetic groups. Cluster 2 tumors are more often adrenal and have a lower metastatic risk. The clusters also differ in hormone-expression patterns.

Genetic background already guides diagnosis and follow-up, which can be tailored by cluster or individual pathogenic variant. Genetic testing is therefore recommended for every person with PPGL. Systemic therapy, however, is not yet personalized.

## Current systemic therapies and prospective trials

New consensus statements are available for SDHD- and SDHB-related disease and for children and adolescents, and a broader guideline for PPGL is in preparation.

Available systemic approaches include somatostatin analogs; radionuclide therapy with peptide receptor radionuclide therapy, or PRRT, and iodine-131 metaiodobenzylguanidine, or MIBG; the HIF-2α inhibitor belzutifan; the tyrosine kinase inhibitors sunitinib and cabozantinib; cyclophosphamide, vincristine, and dacarbazine chemotherapy; and temozolomide.

Belzutifan was approved by the U.S. FDA in May 2025 as the first oral therapy for adults and children aged 12 years and older with locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic PPGL. Approval was based on the LITESPARK-015 study. The reported objective response rate was 26%, median progression-free survival was 22.3 months, and 12-month progression-free survival was 66%. Genetic-subgroup analysis remains of particular interest for VHL- and EPAS1/HIF-2α-mutated tumors.

There are three prospective phase 2 trials of sunitinib, including FIRSTMAPPP, the first randomized, placebo-controlled trial in this disease. In the trial, median progression-free survival was reported as 8.9 months with sunitinib versus 3.6 months with placebo. The speaker reported objective response rates of 36% and 8.3%, respectively. In the cluster 1 SDHB-mutated subgroup, the response rate was 50%.

The single-arm phase 2 Natalie Trial evaluated cabozantinib. Median progression-free survival was reported as 16.6 months and the overall response rate as 25%. Again, response appeared higher in patients with SDHB-mutated tumors, at 60%.

High-specific-activity iodine-131 MIBG, the first FDA-approved systemic therapy for PPGL, was discontinued in 2024. A prospective phase 2 trial of lutetium-177 DOTATATE PRRT reported median progression-free survival of approximately 24 months in apparently sporadic tumors and 12.9 months in SDHx-mutated tumors. Objective response rates were 22% and 33%, respectively.

A prospective phase 2 temozolomide trial published in 2024 reported an objective response rate of 21%. In that study, SDHB status did not predict treatment response.

Overall, PPGL is a rare, heterogeneous disease with limited systemic options and few prospective trials. Only one randomized, placebo-controlled trial has been completed. Preclinical models are also limited, and there are no reliable, broadly available human PPGL cell lines suitable for drug screening.

## Patient-derived PPGL primary-culture platform

We therefore established a personalized drug-screening platform using fresh tumors from patients undergoing surgery. The platform includes PPGLs and gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, and we receive approximately 30 tumors per year.

We use surgical tissue to establish patient-derived primary cultures. For PPGLs, we perform genetic testing and molecular-cluster assignment in parallel. We correlate the patient's clinical features and predictive kinase-activation patterns with the drug-screening results.

To date, we have screened more than 50 drugs. They include chemotherapies, signaling inhibitors, and DNA-damage-response inhibitors. We also repurpose drugs such as the bone-targeted agent zoledronic acid and antidiabetic therapies. The platform can assess individualized risks from factors such as elevated sex hormones, cannabinoids, and endocrine disruptors.

## Cluster-dependent drug responses

We have identified cluster-dependent differences in response to several treatments.

Cabozantinib had significantly greater efficacy in cluster 1 than in cluster 2 primary cultures, including high efficacy in metastatic SDHB-mutated tumors. This parallels the higher response reported in SDHB-mutated participants in the Natalie Trial.

The HIF-2α inhibitor belzutifan showed high efficacy in an EPAS1/HIF-2α-mutated primary culture. Retrospective reports in patients with EPAS1/HIF-2α-mutated PPGL have described an overall response rate of 80%.

We also evaluated the GLP-1 receptor agonist semaglutide. After 72 hours of exposure, semaglutide showed significant antitumor activity in cluster 1 PPGL cultures but no significant aggregate effect in cluster 2 cultures. In tumors with germline RET pathogenic variants associated with MEN2A or MEN2B, higher-dose semaglutide promoted tumor-cell viability. This was not observed in the one culture with a somatic RET variant.

This is relevant because GLP-1 receptor agonist labeling has warned about medullary thyroid carcinoma based on rodent C-cell hyperplasia and tumor data, with precautions or contraindications for patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2. Our findings provide additional preclinical evidence for caution in MEN2-associated PPGL.

Metformin showed antitumor effects in cluster 1, cluster 2, mixed cluster 1/2, and unclassified PPGL cultures. Studying antidiabetic therapies is relevant because people with PPGL often have long-term glycemic dysregulation. Previous data showed higher mean hemoglobin A1c and a higher prevalence of hyperglycemic disorders in people with a history of PPGL than in controls with nonfunctioning adrenal adenomas. Glycemic dysregulation may be both a risk factor for and a long-term consequence of PPGL.

## Cannabidiol and sex-hormone risk assessment

We used the platform for individualized risk assessment. We evaluated elevated sex hormones, which may be relevant during pregnancy or hormone therapy, and cannabinoids, particularly cannabidiol, or CBD.

At clinically relevant concentrations, cluster 2 PPGL cultures showed a significantly greater antitumor response to CBD than cluster 1 cultures. NF1-mutated cluster 2 tumors showed particularly strong responses. The broader dataset nevertheless contained variable and sometimes opposing individual responses, emphasizing the need for patient-specific testing rather than a general assumption of benefit.

Estradiol and progesterone produced significant overall antitumor effects in the PPGL primary cultures. Their efficacy was greatest in tumors with NF1 pathogenic variants. Immunohistochemistry evaluated estrogen, progesterone, and androgen receptors. Estrogen receptor-α positivity was detected in 11 PPGLs, including all four head-and-neck paragangliomas. Estrogen receptor-positive tumors showed a significant reduction in cell viability after estradiol exposure.

In summary, prospective systemic-therapy trials and new consensus statements are now available, but large randomized trials remain extremely challenging. Reliable human cell-line models are lacking. Our patient-derived primary-culture platform permits personalized drug testing, repurposing, and risk assessment.

We identified cluster-dependent responses to cabozantinib, pazopanib, GLP-1 receptor agonists, sex hormones, and CBD. These findings may help identify genetically driven treatment options and individual risks of tumor-promoting effects in people with PPGL.

Thank you for your attention. I think I am a little under time.

## Questions and discussion

**Moderator:** Thank you, Dr. Wang. We have time for questions. Please come to the microphones.

**Audience member:** Thank you for a fascinating talk. When you perform drug screening, do the concentrations to which you expose the cells account for what can actually be achieved in patients?

**Katharina Wang:** We use clinically relevant concentrations, or doses close to clinically relevant concentrations, based on plasma concentrations achieved in patients. We usually test either a median clinically attainable concentration or the maximum attainable concentration in our primary cultures.

**Moderator:** What has been the biggest surprise from the platform? Have you observed a response that would not have been predicted by the tumor's genetic profile alone?

**Katharina Wang:** When we established the platform, we hoped to enable personalized therapy and stratification for people with metastatic PPGL, particularly after progression on standard systemic therapies or when treatment options were limited. Over recent years, we have identified more and more genuinely cluster-dependent differences. The extent of those differences between clusters 1 and 2 has been both interesting and somewhat surprising.

**Lauren Fishbein, University of Colorado:** I am interested in your hypothesis about NF1 mutations and the effects of estradiol and progesterone. Twenty-five or thirty years ago, my PhD work examined estrogen and progesterone effects on neurofibroma growth in people with germline NF1 variants. Those hormones increased proliferation in primary neurofibroma cultures. Do you have a hypothesis for the interaction between these hormones and NF1 variants in PPGL? Were your NF1 variants somatic or germline?

**Katharina Wang:** They were mostly somatic. We are continuing to study the mechanisms. Some preclinical breast-cancer data have shown increased sensitivity of NF1-mutated cells to higher-dose estradiol, but we do not yet know the mechanism in PPGL. CBD and estradiol may affect some common signaling pathways, which could explain why both showed greater efficacy in NF1-mutated tumors. We are still studying the detailed mechanisms for both.

**Lauren Fishbein:** Thank you.

**Moderator:** With that, we will end the symposium. Thank you very much for attending so late in the day.
