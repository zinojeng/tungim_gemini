## Rationale for dual melanocortin-receptor agonism

**Jillian L. Seiler:** Good morning—or almost good afternoon. Thank you for the opportunity to present this work.

Current obesity treatments such as incretins have transformed care, but some patient populations remain unable to use these therapies, and adverse effects remain important. We also need treatments that provide healthier weight loss. At Endevica Bio, we believe that the melanocortin system, a central regulator of appetite and metabolism, provides an alternative therapeutic approach.

Previous attempts to target the melanocortin system in general obesity largely focused on melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R). We were also interested in melanocortin-3 receptor (MC3R), which is expressed centrally and may provide additional therapeutic effects. We first asked about the individual contributions of MC3R and MC4R and then examined what happens when both are activated.

## Selective and combined agonism in nonhuman primates

Our chemistry team developed selective MC3R and MC4R agonists. We first tested these in an acute homeostatic challenge. Lean male nonhuman primates were fasted overnight and given a single dose of one of three treatments. MC3R agonism produced the greatest suppression of the refeeding response among the individual receptors, while combined MC3R/MC4R agonism outperformed both mono-agonists.

We next studied a chronic state of challenged homeostasis in male nonhuman primates with diet-induced obesity on a high-fat diet. Selective MC3R agonism changed body weight very little and, if anything, produced a slight increase. As expected, MC4R agonism decreased body weight, and combined agonism again outperformed both individual approaches.

Body-composition analysis helped explain the MC3R result. Although the treatments decreased fat mass, selective MC3R agonism increased lean mass. Across the acute and chronic settings, the dual agonist outperformed the mono-agonists.

These findings led to the development of 710GO, an orally available dual MC3R/MC4R agonist. In male nonhuman primates with diet-induced obesity, chronic oral administration produced approximately 11.8% weight loss over 15 weeks relative to vehicle. We observed no gastrointestinal adverse effects, limited weight rebound after treatment cessation, and relative preservation of lean mass.

We therefore believe that the melanocortin system—and dual MC3R/MC4R agonism in particular—offers an important alternative approach to obesity treatment.

## Audience Q&A

**Audience member:** MC4R mutations cause obesity. Are you suggesting that the broader population with general obesity could also be treated by targeting these receptors?

**Jillian L. Seiler:** Yes. Obesity is heterogeneous, and people with MC4R mutations are particularly sensitive to melanocortin-based treatment. By targeting both receptors, we believe we can achieve a broader therapeutic window and greater melanocortin-system stimulation for general obesity.

**Audience member:** Did these studies include both males and females, and do you know whether gonadal steroids influence the effect?

**Jillian L. Seiler:** The studies presented here included males only. We are very interested in sex differences. We have studied them mainly in mice and rats so far, and we do see differences. We are pursuing this because we believe the melanocortin system interacts with steroid hormones and estrogen-related conditions.

**Audience member:** What about increased blood pressure when treating general obesity?

**Jillian L. Seiler:** We have not observed changes in blood pressure. We used telemetry in nonhuman primates at supratherapeutic doses and did not see those increases. We believe the molecule's design has moved us away from that liability. This work has been published.

Thank you.
