## Outstanding Clinical Investigator Award

**Carol A. Lange:** Our next award is the Outstanding Clinical Investigator Award, which honors an internationally recognized investigator who has contributed significantly to understanding the pathogenesis and therapy of endocrine and metabolic diseases.

This year's recipient is Dr. Samuel Klein. Dr. Klein is the William H. Danforth Professor of Medicine at Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis, Missouri.

He uses a combination of sophisticated basic and clinical science research techniques to address clinically relevant questions in human subjects, which are ultimately directed toward improving human health and patient care.

His research is focused on understanding the cellular and multi-organ-system physiological mechanisms responsible for the heterogeneity in metabolic dysfunction associated with obesity, prediabetes, diabetes, and the therapeutic effects of weight loss. He has also conducted important clinical trials that evaluated the efficacy and metabolic effects of weight-reduction therapies.

Please welcome to the stage Dr. Klein.

## Acceptance remarks

**Samuel Klein:** Thank you very much. This is obviously a great honor. And one of the nice things about getting an award like this is that it makes you think about the people who were really involved in helping you be productive in your career.

And so what I want to do now—which will be boring for you, but interesting for me—is really thank some of the people who were responsible for helping me develop my career and for some of the contributions I've been able to make.

The first thing is really to thank Rexford S. Ahima and Ralph A. DeFronzo for nominating me for the award; Robert R. Wolfe, who was my mentor and a very generous and insightful mentor in launching my research career; and, of course, all the wonderful colleagues I've been fortunate to work with.

But particularly, I want to thank Bruce W. Patterson and Gordon I. Smith, who were at Washington University and with whom I worked for many, many years; and the research team—the coordinators, dietitians, fellows, and nurse coordinators—who really helped conduct all the studies.

And finally, my family, of course, who have been very patient and supportive throughout my time doing research.

So thank you very much to the Society for this award, and to all the people to whom I owe this award.
