## Introduction and objective

Good morning. My name is Vania Balderrama-Brondani. I am a postdoctoral fellow at MD Anderson Cancer Center, and today I will present our experience with extended survival in metastatic adrenocortical carcinoma, focused on patients who achieved long-term survival despite advanced disease.

Adrenocortical carcinoma, or ACC, is a rare and aggressive malignancy. Prognosis is strongly dependent on disease stage, and outcomes are particularly poor once metastatic disease develops. The objective of our study was to characterize patients with metastatic ACC who survived 10 years or longer after diagnosis of metastasis.

## Methods and cohort selection

We conducted a retrospective, single-institution study. We included adult patients with ACC who survived more than 10 years after developing metastatic disease, and patients with insufficient clinical data were excluded.

In our initial cohort of 644 patients with ACC, we identified 298 patients who developed metastatic disease during follow-up and an additional 197 patients classified as stage IV at initial diagnosis. Twenty patients from the metastatic group and seven patients from the stage IV group formed the focus of our analysis.

## Patient and disease characteristics

Sixty-three percent of the total of 27 patients were female, with a median age at diagnosis of 42 years. Forty percent of tumors were hormonally active, most commonly cortisol-producing. The most frequent sites of metastasis were the liver, followed by the abdomen/peritoneum, lungs, and bone.

Thirteen patients achieved an R0 resection at the time of adrenalectomy. In the metastatic setting, patients received multimodality treatment. Nearly 80% received mitotane, and 63% received platinum-based chemotherapy.

## Conclusions and future directions

In conclusion, ACC is a highly aggressive malignancy, and in our cohort we observed a substantial metastatic burden affecting 77% of our patients. Importantly, our findings demonstrate a survival benefit associated with individualized multimodal treatment in the metastatic setting. This supports the role of combining and tailoring therapies as a critical component of patient care rather than using a one-size-fits-all approach.

Finally, although uncommon, we show that long-term survival is achievable in a subset of patients with metastatic ACC. Understanding why some patients achieve extended survival may help us optimize treatment sequencing and improve outcomes in this rare disease. To better identify the predictors, a multicenter collaboration across the A5 centers is currently underway.
