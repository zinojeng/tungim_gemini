#### Ismaa Sadaf Farooqi, MD, PhD — Gerald D. Aurbach Award for Outstanding Translational Research

**Dr. Lange:** The Gerald D. Aurbach Award for Outstanding Translational Research recognizes outstanding contributions to research that accelerates the transition of scientific discoveries into clinical applications.
I'm pleased to present this award to Dr. Ismaa Sadaf Farooqi. Dr. Farooqi is distinguished for her discoveries of fundamental mechanisms that control human energy homeostasis. With colleagues, she discovered the first genes whose disruption causes severe obesity. In pioneering clinical studies, she established that the principal driver of human obesity is a failure of the central control of appetite, and that the leptin-melanocortin pathway regulates food intake, macronutrient preference, and food reward and body weight.

In children with congenital leptin deficiency treated with recombinant leptin, she showed that leptin reduced hyperphagia, permitted the onset of puberty at an appropriate developmental stage, and reversed T cell-mediated immune dysfunction. This work has shaped our understanding of how physiological states characterized by a fall in leptin levels, such as starvation, the weight-reduced state, and pathological states characterized by low leptin levels, such as anorexia nervosa or lipodystrophies, impact on reproduction and immunity. Her work is an exemplar of how understanding disease mechanisms can change. Diagnostic practice informed the development of mechanism-based therapies and improved the lives of patients.

Genetic investigation is now part of the diagnostic evaluation of severe childhood onset obesity recommended in clinical guidelines globally. Dr. Farooqi has played a leading role in the design and conduct of clinical trials of medicines that enhance signaling through the leptin-melanocortin pathway. As a result, subgroups of children with severe, life-threatening obesity can now be treated efficiently and effectively with therapies which have been licensed. By demonstrating that severe childhood obesity can represent a medical disorder, her work has prevented children from being removed from their families and placed into social care due to concerns about neglect.

Alongside her scientific contributions, Dr. Farooqi has been actively involved in public engagement and advocacy, leveraging the knowledge derived from her translational research to encourage a more sympathetic understanding of the challenges faced by people with severe obesity. Dr. Farooqi, we look forward to hearing more about your fascinating work later today.
