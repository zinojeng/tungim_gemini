## Background

Hi, everyone. Thank you for the opportunity to present today. We do not have any disclosures.

Adrenocortical carcinoma, or ACC, is a very rare malignancy. Although it is rare, it is very aggressive. The median overall survival is three to four years. Patients who have distant metastasis or stage IV disease have very low five-year survival rates of 15%.

Genetic mutations or alterations, such as in TP53 and IGF2, have been reported as precursors for the development of adrenocortical carcinomas. The Wnt/β-catenin pathway is one of the most extensively studied pathways in the literature, and it has been reported to be associated with the pathogenesis and development of adrenocortical carcinomas in many studies. However, other signaling pathways, such as Hippo, cell cycle, TP53, and MYC, remain unexplored, and there are limited data describing their impact on patient survival or clinical outcomes.

## Study aims and methods

The aim of our study was to look at the association between those signaling pathways and survival outcomes in patients with ACC. We then specifically looked at a subpopulation of patients who had alterations in TP53 and MYC. We investigated their genetic profiles and also evaluated how this might affect their clinical and survival outcomes.

To achieve our goals, we extracted data for these patients from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Patients with available genomic, recurrence, metastatic, and survival data were included in our study. We performed Kaplan–Meier analyses and assessed five-year survival outcomes in these patients.

We also performed gene set enrichment analysis, or GSEA. This is a statistical method that evaluates how alterations in a group or set of genes can affect the biology or function of cells.

## Overall cohort and pathway-level survival analysis

Table 1 represents our overall cohort. We had a total of 92 patients who underwent adrenalectomy for ACC. The median age at the time of surgery was almost 50 years. The majority of patients were female, non-Hispanic, and White. Most patients underwent R0 resection, which means there was no residual disease after surgery. Only about 20% had distant metastasis, or stage IV disease.

The next table represents all 10 signaling pathways available in our dataset. Only alterations in either the MYC pathway or TP53 were associated with worse survival outcomes compared with the other signaling pathways. To try to understand, or find a possible reason for, this observation, we decided to specifically investigate the subpopulation of patients who had alterations in those pathways.

## TP53 and MYC alteration subgroups

The subgroup table represents our patient cohorts. Twenty patients had alterations in both TP53 and MYC, 27 had alterations in TP53 only, 14 had alterations in MYC only, and 30 had no alterations in any of these signaling pathways.

At baseline, there was no significant difference in patient demographics, including age, sex, race, or ethnicity. The most important finding I want to highlight is that patients who had combined alterations in TP53 and MYC had higher rates of recurrence and distant metastasis. We also performed a post hoc analysis, which confirmed that the significant difference shown here was mainly driven by this cohort of patients.

Most patients underwent R0 resection, but the percentage was lower in the combined-alteration group than in the other cohorts. To determine whether combined TP53 and MYC alterations still affected tumor characteristics after complete surgical resection, we performed a subanalysis in patients who underwent R0 resection. Patients whose tumors had alterations in both TP53 and MYC still had higher rates of recurrence than the other cohorts.

## Five-year survival outcomes

We then performed Kaplan–Meier analysis. The first graph represents five-year survival outcomes in our overall cohort. Patients with dual TP53 and MYC alterations had worse five-year survival outcomes than the other cohorts.

To investigate this further, we also performed survival analysis in patients who underwent R0 resection. The graph shows that patients with combined MYC and TP53 alterations still had worse survival outcomes even after complete surgical resection with no residual disease and no positive margin.

To summarize this part, patients with dual MYC and TP53 alterations had aggressive tumor features and worse survival outcomes, even after R0 resection. To try to understand why, we decided to examine their genomic profiles and determine whether the genetic alterations could be a driving factor for these findings.

## Gene-expression findings

The volcano plot functions as a filter that shows changes in gene expression. The red area on the right represents upregulation of genes in dual-altered samples compared with the other cohorts, while the blue area represents downregulation of genes in the dual-altered group. The y-axis represents statistical significance; the higher the point, the more significant it is.

We found upregulation of genes mainly involved in cellular proliferation, evasion of the immune response, and increased inflammation. In contrast, genes involved in mitochondrial metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, protein folding, or maintenance of cellular architecture were downregulated.

We then assessed whether a similar pattern remained when the dual-altered group was compared with the TP53-only group, and we observed a similar trend. Genes such as GAB2 and 5T4 were upregulated and were associated with increased cellular proliferation, while genes involved in maintaining mitochondrial function and oxidative phosphorylation were downregulated.

When we compared the dual-altered group with the MYC-only group, we again had similar findings. The upregulated genes were associated with increased proliferation, while the downregulated genes were associated with mitochondrial dysfunction.

## Gene set enrichment analysis

To understand how the individual gene changes could affect cell biology and function, we performed the gene set enrichment analysis described earlier. This analysis examines whether alterations in groups of genes that have different names but share the same biological function affect cellular function.

We examined up to 20 pathways. Red represents pathways that were upregulated and therefore more likely to be enriched, while blue represents pathways that were downregulated. The next figure summarizes 12 pathways.

Pathways such as E2F targets, which regulate the cell cycle; the G2/M checkpoint, which regulates progression from the G2 phase to the M phase; and inflammatory pathways were upregulated. In contrast, oxidative phosphorylation—which is involved in mitochondrial function, maintenance of aerobic metabolism, and ATP production—was downregulated, along with other pathways that regulate cellular function.

## Conclusions

To summarize our findings, ACC tumors with dual TP53 and MYC alterations had dysregulation of genes mainly involved in cellular proliferation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and protein misfolding. These alterations could provide a possible rationale for why these patients were more likely to have increased recurrence and distant metastasis.

This may also open future avenues for investigations of therapeutic strategies or targets addressing the proliferative and metabolic vulnerabilities in patients with dual TP53 and MYC alterations. Thank you. We have time for questions.
