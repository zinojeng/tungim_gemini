### Why target more than one obesity pathway?

**Dr. Burkhardt da Silveira:** Good morning, everyone. Imagine trying to lose weight while your own biology is working against you. This is the reality for millions of people dealing with obesity every day. It is not a matter of lack of willpower. Obesity is a chronic disease with complex signaling pathways that regulate hunger, satiety, metabolism, and energy balance.

We have made incredible advances in the field with medications such as semaglutide. But even with those medications, we still have patients who are unable to achieve the weight loss they need or who struggle with weight regain.

That leads to our first question: what if we target more than one pathway at the same time? That is the idea behind **CagriSema**, a combination of **cagrilintide**, a long-acting amylin analogue, and **semaglutide**, a long-acting glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist.

That leads to our second question: should this combination provide additional benefits, and most importantly, at what safety cost? That was the goal of our study.

### Systematic review and meta-analysis

We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis that included four randomized controlled trials and 4,751 participants. We compared CagriSema with placebo, cagrilintide alone, and semaglutide alone.

The results were striking. Compared with placebo, CagriSema demonstrated approximately 14 percentage points greater weight loss; the results slide reports a mean difference of **−13.85 percentage points**. Compared with cagrilintide monotherapy and semaglutide monotherapy, CagriSema demonstrated approximately 8 percentage points greater weight loss; the slide reports mean differences of **−7.97** and **−7.76 percentage points**, respectively.

The numbers do not stop at the scale. Compared with placebo, CagriSema demonstrated an approximately 5.5 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure; the slide reports **−5.53 mmHg**.

### Adverse events and conclusion

Those benefits matter only if the medication is tolerable. CagriSema was associated with more adverse events than placebo, especially gastrointestinal adverse events. However, compared with cagrilintide monotherapy and semaglutide monotherapy, there was no statistically significant increase in adverse events. We therefore have a medication that provides additional weight loss with a safety profile comparable to monotherapy in the included trials.

This tells us that perhaps the future treatment of obesity will not rely only on targeting a single pathway. If we have a multifactorial disease, perhaps our treatment should be multifactorial too. Thank you.
