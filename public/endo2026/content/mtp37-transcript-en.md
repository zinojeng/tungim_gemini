### Key learning points

So the key learning points: when screening for AI, it's important to recognise the physiological cortisol circadian rhythm, which is quite profound. Random cortisol levels on the whole are of no value. ACTH stimulation tests can be done at any time, except if you consider that there's been a recent onset of adrenal insufficiency. Early morning cortisol and waking salivary cortisone are good screening tests. Oral oestrogens raise CBG, so use waking salivary cortisone if it's available. It's important to diagnose the cause of AI, and then, so having a paired cortisol and ACTH is very useful.

The commonest cause of tertiary AI is glucocorticoid-induced. Adrenal crisis requires immediate treatment. You can't go wrong with 100 mg of hydrocortisone. All patients with AI should be given an emergency pack and taught the importance of stress dosing. And fatigue is common in patients with AI, but I think there are new treatment regimens that we can use which may improve this.
